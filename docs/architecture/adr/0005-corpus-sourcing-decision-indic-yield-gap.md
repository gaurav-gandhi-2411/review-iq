# ADR 0005: Corpus Sourcing Decision — Closing (or Accepting) the Indic Yield Gap

**Status:** Proposed — this is a decision request, not an accepted decision. GG decides; nothing here is committed or executed.
**Date:** 2026-07-31
**Scope:** Amendment to ADR 0004. ADR 0004 stood up the corpus-mining pipeline and named the gap; this ADR prices the options for closing it, as requested, without committing to one.

## Context

ADR 0004 measured the real blocker precisely: the Indic-strata (hi-en + hi combined) eval-set yield from the three cleared, ingested Flipkart Kaggle sources is **595 records — 12% of the 2,000 floor** needed to resolve the spec §2 pre-registered quality-moat decision rule at a trustworthy MDE (595 records: MDE 6.5–8.1pp, wider than the rule's own 2–5pp margins; 2,000 floor: MDE 3.5pp; 5,000 target: MDE 2.2pp). ADR 0004 named this the single largest blocker to Wave 3 and explicitly deferred the sourcing decision rather than guessing at it. This ADR is that follow-up, priced across five options per the standing instruction (a fifth, opt-in customer data contribution, added 2026-07-31 per Wave 1 S0/P1-P3 remediation), with one additional constraint stated up front:

**Ruled out categorically, not priced as an option:** synthetic review generation as a corpus source. Training an authenticity/extraction system on LLM-generated synthetic reviews to later detect real ones is a distribution-mismatch trap — the model would learn the generating model's stylistic fingerprint, not genuine review structure, and authenticity scoring specifically would be measuring "does this look like GPT/Llama output" rather than "is this a real customer review." This applies even though `corpus_pipeline/adversarial_pairs.py` (ADR 0004) generates synthetic *fake* reviews — those are explicitly held out from training entirely (tagged, directory-isolated), which is the opposite of what "using synthetic data as a corpus source" would mean here.

## Research performed for this amendment (live, 2026-07-31 — not from memory)

Searched for Hindi/Hinglish e-commerce review datasets and commercial Indic data-collection vendors. Real findings, cited:

**Public sources found — none close the gap directly:**
- **L3Cube-HingCorpus** (52.93M Hindi-English code-mixed sentences, [arXiv:2204.08398](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.08398)) — huge scale, but Twitter-scraped social media, not product reviews. Wrong domain/register for extraction fine-tuning ground truth; could plausibly help a *language-model pretraining* step but not a labeled-extraction corpus.
- **PHINC** (13.7K Hinglish sentences), **IIIT-H en-hi-codemixed-corpus** (6K), **IIITH Hindi-English sentiment** (3,879 comments) — all real, permissively available academic corpora, but each individually and combined (~24K) is far short of useful volume even before filtering for review-domain content, and none are review-domain (social media / generic sentiment).
- **BanglishRev** ([huggingface.co/datasets/BanglishRev](https://huggingface.co/datasets/BanglishRev/bangla-english-and-code-mixed-ecommerce-review-dataset), 1.74M reviews) — the closest structural match to what we need (a large code-mixed *e-commerce review* dataset) — but it's **Bengali**-English, not Hindi-English. Confirms the pattern (large code-mixed review corpora *can* exist) without providing usable data for this project's language pair.
- **IberaSoft ecommerce-reviews-sentiment** (20K reviews, CC BY 4.0, 2026) — real and permissively licensed, but not Hindi-specific (Amazon/Yelp/SaaS reviews, predominantly English) — would need filtering for Hindi/Hinglish content, and based on ADR 0004's own finding on Flipkart sources ("genuine Hinglish candidates are typically 50-200 out of 200K rows"), the realistic yield from a 20K-row non-Hindi-targeted source is likely single-digit-to-low-double-digit records — not worth the ingestion/licensing-verification overhead on its own.

**No dataset combining (Hindi or Hinglish) + (product review domain) + (adequate volume) was found publicly available.** This is not "we didn't look hard enough" — [AI4Bharat's IndicNLP catalog](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/indicnlp_catalog), the most comprehensive public index of Indic-language NLP resources, was also checked and does not list one either. This is a genuine, structural gap in the public data landscape, not a search-effort problem.

**Commercial/licensed data vendors — real, but pricing not publicly listed:**
- **Shaip** ([shaip.com/solutions/indian-language-datasets](https://www.shaip.com/solutions/indian-language-datasets/)) — offers custom Indic-language data collection/annotation, including Hindi-English code-switched data, with flexible licensing/ownership terms. No public per-record or per-project pricing; requires direct sales contact for a quote.
- **Twine** — a marketplace for commissioning custom datasets, including Hindi-language work; similarly no public pricing.
- Neither vendor's pricing is stated here as a number because none is publicly available — reporting a specific $/record figure without a real quote would violate this project's own standing rule against presenting an estimate as if measured. The only honest cost figure for this option is "unknown until a vendor conversation happens," with an industry-context range for planning purposes only, explicitly labeled as such below.

## The five options, priced

### (a) Additional permissively-licensed public sources
- **Expected yield:** Low-to-marginal. Given the research above, there is no untapped large public source combining the right language and domain. Best case: continued scanning of Kaggle/HuggingFace for small (hundreds-to-low-thousands-row) Hindi/Hinglish review or review-adjacent datasets as they're published, filtered the same way the existing 3 Flipkart sources were (expect a similar ~0.1–0.5% genuine-vernacular yield rate per ADR 0004's own finding). Realistic estimate: a few hundred additional records over continued effort, not the 1,400+ needed to close the floor gap (2,000 − 595).
- **Cost:** $0 (public data).
- **Calendar time:** Ongoing, low-intensity (1–2 weeks of periodic scanning is a reasonable bound before diminishing returns are clear) — this is cheap to *keep doing* in parallel with any other option, not an either/or.
- **USP claim supported:** If it somehow worked, the full quality-moat claim (spec §2: "measurably better... on Hinglish/Hindi extraction"). Given the low expected yield, treat this as a **background/ongoing activity, not a plan that closes the gap on its own.**

### (b) Licensed/purchased datasets
- **Expected yield:** Potentially closes the gap directly — commercial data vendors sell custom collection at whatever volume is budgeted for.
- **Cost:** **Not established — no real quote obtained.** For planning purposes only (explicitly an industry-context estimate, not a quote, and must not be treated as a real number until a vendor conversation happens): specialized low-resource-language data collection/annotation services commonly run from roughly $0.50 to $5+ per labeled record depending on complexity and required QA, which would put a 2,000–5,000-record custom Hindi/Hinglish review collection somewhere in the very rough $1,000–$25,000 range — a wide, low-confidence band stated only to give a sense of order of magnitude, not a budget number. Getting a real number requires directly contacting Shaip, Twine, or a similar vendor with this project's exact spec (volume, domain, language, QA bar).
- **Calendar time:** Typically weeks to a couple of months for a custom vendor engagement (scoping, collection, QA, delivery) — industry-typical, not a quote.
- **USP claim supported:** Full quality-moat claim, at real (currently unquantified) cost and calendar time — and it introduces a new dependency (a paid vendor relationship) into what has otherwise been a fully self-funded, public-data pipeline.

### (c) Narrow the claim to fewer languages at adequate n
- **Mechanism:** The spec's own USP language treats Hinglish as the headline differentiator ("the only review-intelligence API... measurably better than general frontier models on Hinglish/Hindi extraction" — Hinglish named first, and arguably the harder, more genuinely differentiated NLP problem versus pure Hindi, which general multilingual frontier models likely handle more competently already). Narrowing the Wave 3 quality-moat claim to **Hinglish only** (dropping pure-Hindi Devanagari from that specific claim, while pure-Hindi extraction continues to ship as-is via the existing shipped prompt, just without a *superiority* claim attached) means the full 2,000–5,000 target applies to Hinglish alone instead of being split across hi-en + hi combined.
- **Expected yield:** **Needs a real number before this is actionable** — this ADR does not have the current hi-en-only vs. hi-only split of the 595-record baseline (not present in this worktree; `benchmark/vernacular_v2/`'s isolation output should be re-queried directly for the exact split before treating this option as sized). If the current 595 is roughly evenly split, this option alone doesn't close the gap either — it would still need new hi-en-specific sourcing, just against a lower target than "hi-en + hi combined." Directionally cheaper than combined, not free.
- **Cost:** $0 in isolation (a scoping decision), though it doesn't eliminate the need for *some* additional sourcing under (a) or (b) unless the current split turns out to already be Hinglish-heavy.
- **Calendar time:** Immediate for the scoping decision itself; whatever sourcing work remains is priced under (a)/(b).
- **USP claim supported:** A narrower but still genuinely defensible claim ("measurably better on Hinglish specifically") — arguably a *cleaner* positioning than the combined claim, not just a fallback, since Hinglish code-mixing is the harder and more differentiated capability.

### (d) Accept the cost-moat branch of the pre-registered decision rule
- **Mechanism:** Per the spec's own pre-registered rule (§2): if the fine-tune lands within 2–5pp of the 70B teacher at ≥10× lower cost/latency, ship as a **cost/economics + VPC-deployability** moat instead of a quality-superiority moat. This doesn't eliminate the need for real Indic eval data — the decision rule still needs to be resolved with *some* MDE precision — but it changes what's being claimed (parity within a band, not superiority), which is a philosophically different and arguably lower bar than "measurably better."
- **Expected yield / gap:** Even the wider 2–5pp band isn't automatically resolved by the current 595 records — the MDE table in ADR 0004 shows 595 records sits at 6.5–8.1pp MDE, still wider than the 2–5pp band this rule needs to distinguish. This option reduces how much more data is needed, but does not reduce it to zero; the exact reduced target hasn't been computed in this pass (a follow-up MDE calculation targeting resolution of a 2–5pp band specifically, rather than the tighter combined bar, would need to be run before this option can be called "sized").
- **Cost:** $0 new sourcing beyond continuing (a) at background level.
- **Calendar time:** Immediate as a reframing decision; the distillation fine-tune experiment itself still needs to run in Wave 3 regardless of which branch is targeted.
- **USP claim supported:** "Fraction of the per-call cost, deployable inside the customer's own VPC" (spec §2's own alternative framing) — a fundamentally different, still-real USP that doesn't depend on Hindi/Hinglish quality superiority. Notably, this is one of two options (see (e) below) that doesn't require solving the yield gap as a precondition — it changes what the eval needs to prove, not how much data it needs (though it likely needs somewhat less).

### (e) Opt-in customer data contribution — priced 2026-07-31, per Wave 1 S0/P1-P3 remediation

- **Mechanism:** A separate, standalone consent flow — never a pre-checked box, never bundled into ToS/signup acceptance — offering discounted pricing (a specific percentage is a pricing decision, not priced here) in exchange for the customer's explicit agreement that their (anonymized) review text may be contributed to this project's training/eval corpus. Revocable at any time via a dedicated account setting, not buried in a general privacy-settings page.
- **Legal validity — DPDP Act 2023 (India):** live-checked 2026-07-31 (not from memory): the Act does not permit repurposing personal data beyond the originally-consented purpose without **fresh, separate consent** — matching the "never bundled into ToS" design above; that's a legal floor, not just a UX preference. Valid consent under the Act must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous, demonstrated by clear affirmative action. Withdrawal must trigger erasure of the data once consent is withdrawn (subject to the retraining question below). Note on timing: DPDP Rules 2025 were notified 2025-11-13 with a compliance deadline of 2027-05-13 — this project is inside the transition window, not yet under a hard enforcement deadline, but designing to the eventual standard now avoids a scramble later. [Sources: [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=5b0947a9-4f71-4c61-bccc-c2818ef43922), [dpdpa.com](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section4.html)]
- **Legal validity — GDPR (for any EU/UK-domiciled end-customers of Samidha Reviews' own customers, if applicable):** Article 7(4)'s bundling prohibition is the same design constraint as DPDP's fresh-consent requirement — consent conditioned on a contract's performance is not freely given, which is exactly why this must be a separate opt-in, not a ToS clause. Article 17 (right to erasure) applies on withdrawal.
- **Anonymization required for the consent to hold:** review text is free-form and can carry indirect identifiers beyond the structured PII this project's sanitizer already strips (emails, phone numbers, card numbers) — e.g., a named person, a specific purchase date + product combination that's identifiable within a small merchant's own customer base, or a location. True anonymization (irreversible, no realistic re-identification path) would place the data outside GDPR/DPDP scope entirely; **pseudonymization alone (e.g., hashing an identifier) is explicitly NOT sufficient** — GDPR treats pseudonymized data as still personal data. The minimum bar for this option: (1) run the existing PII-redaction pipeline (`app/core/sanitize.py`) as a floor, not the whole answer; (2) strip or generalize any merchant/product context that could re-identify a specific purchase event within a small catalog; (3) consider a publication delay/batching step so a single contributed review can't be time-correlated back to a known transaction. None of this is built today — this is what "required for the consent to hold" would mean if this option is chosen, not a description of current capability.
- **Expected yield per customer per month:** Not fabricated from an assumed customer count — grounded in this project's own already-measured real traffic: Section F's SLO measurement (`scripts/slo_report.py`, live Cloud Logging query) found **n=2 real `llm.extracted` events in a 14-day window, project-wide, across all customers combined** (not per-customer) — i.e., at today's actual traffic level, yield from this option is close to zero regardless of the opt-in rate assumed, because there is barely any base volume to opt in from yet. Formula for projecting forward once traffic grows: `monthly_yield ≈ (extractions/month) × (opt-in rate) × (fraction surviving anonymization filtering)` — plug in real numbers once real customer-growth data exists; this option's usefulness is gated on customer growth succeeding first, not on the consent mechanism itself.
- **Does revocation force retraining?** Live-checked 2026-07-31: **yes, in any legally defensible design.** GDPR Article 17 obliges erasure on withdrawal, and current published research and legal commentary converge on the same practical point: once data has been used to train a neural network, "machine unlearning" techniques exist but **do not carry a theoretical guarantee that the data's influence has been fully removed** — only full retraining that excludes the withdrawn contributor's data carries that guarantee. [Sources: [Cloud Security Alliance](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2025/04/11/the-right-to-be-forgotten-but-can-ai-forget), [ScienceDirect, "Algorithms that forget"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S026736492300095X)]. Practical consequence for this project if this option is chosen: the training pipeline would need to track *which contributed records came from which customer* (so a specific customer's contribution can be excluded), and treat "a customer revoked" as a trigger for the *next* training run to exclude them — not as a promise that an *already-trained* model is retroactively scrubbed. This is a real operational cost this option adds beyond the ones priced in (a)-(d) above, and should be weighed against the (likely small, per the yield estimate above) near-term data volume this option would realistically produce.
- **Cost:** $0 direct spend; the operational cost is the per-customer discount (a pricing decision, not priced here) plus the anonymization-pipeline and revocation-tracking engineering work described above, neither of which exists today.
- **Calendar time:** Not gated on external vendors (unlike (b)) — gated on this project's own engineering time to build the consent flow, anonymization filtering, and per-contributor tracking, plus (as the yield estimate shows) on customer growth actually happening first.
- **USP claim supported:** None directly — this is a corpus-growth mechanism, not itself a customer-facing differentiator, though a customer-facing "help us build better Indic-language support, get a discount" framing could be a minor secondary marketing angle if GG wants it.
- **Do not implement.** Priced and documented per the standing instruction only — no code, no consent flow, no anonymization pipeline built in this pass.

## Decision

**Not made here.** GG decides. Options are not mutually exclusive — (a) can run as a low-cost background activity regardless of which of (b)/(c)/(d)/(e) is also chosen; (c) and (d) can be combined (narrow the language claim AND accept a wider precision band); (e) is realistically a *background, slow-growing* supplement to whichever of (a)-(d) resolves the near-term gap, given its yield is currently gated on customer growth this project doesn't yet have. Recommended next step, if useful: get a real quote from one commercial vendor (option b) to replace this ADR's rough-order-of-magnitude range with a real number, since that's the one option whose cost is currently a genuine unknown rather than a computable one — but even proposing that step is offered as a suggestion, not a decision made on GG's behalf.

## Consequences

- Wave 3 cannot responsibly begin the quality-moat branch of the pre-registered decision rule until this is resolved one way or another — training against an eval set with 6.5–8.1pp MDE and calling a ≤2pp result "resolved" would itself be a rule violation of the "never widen a tolerance to make a check pass" standard applied everywhere else in this project.
- This ADR does not block option (a) from continuing in the background — low-cost, no decision required, already effectively "yes" by default per ADR 0004's own pipeline being ready to ingest new sources the moment they're found and license-cleared.
- If (d) is chosen, the pre-registered decision rule (spec §2) itself may need a companion ADR amendment stating the specific MDE the reduced target needs to resolve *within the cost-moat framing specifically* — not computed in this pass, flagged as the concrete next step if GG leans toward (d).

## Alternatives considered

- **Do nothing, proceed to Wave 3 training anyway at n=595.** Rejected — directly violates the pre-registered decision rule's own stated margins; this is exactly the "unfalsifiable claim" pattern spec §2 explicitly warns against ("No marketing claim is written before the number exists").
- **Synthetic review generation as a corpus source.** Rejected categorically per the standing instruction and the distribution-mismatch reasoning above — not priced as an option.
