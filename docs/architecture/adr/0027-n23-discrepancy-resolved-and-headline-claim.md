# ADR 0027: The n=23 "0/10 / 1/12" discrepancy has no in-repo source; the headline claim for launch

## Context

Session 12 (ADR 0026) computed coverage / accuracy-on-answered / wrong-committed at n=106 and
flagged, rather than resolved, that it could not reconcile the brief's cited n=23 figures
("buy_again 0/10 wrong-committed, sentiment 1/12") against this repo's own published n=23 numbers
(ADR 0018). Session 13 P1a asks for the discrepancy to be resolved and the cause reported. This
number also gates the landing page, pricing page, and outreach email (P1b/P6a), so it needs a
final, evidence-backed answer before anything customer-facing is written against it.

## Method

Exhaustive search for a source of "0/10" / "1/12" as wrong-committed counts, before concluding
none exists:

- `grep -rn "0/10\|1/12"` across `docs/`, `eval/`, `PLAN.md` — the only hits are ADR 0026's own
  discussion of the discrepancy and unrelated literal `10/10` review-text substrings inside
  fixture files (e.g. `"Great headphones. 10/10."`).
- `git log --all -S "0/10"` and `-S "1/12"` across the full history of every branch this repo has
  ever had (not just `main`) — no commit ever introduced a file containing either string in a
  wrong-committed context.
- Re-read ADR 0018 (the actual n=23 measurement) in full: coverage 43.5%/82.6%, accuracy-on-
  answered 70.0%/89.5% for buy_again/sentiment. Reconciling those percentages against n=23 implies
  denominators of 10 and 19 answered (not 12) with 3 and 2 wrong (not 0 and 1) — a real, concrete
  arithmetic mismatch, not a rounding artifact.
- Checked whether a *different* corpus or process might be the real source: the 2-judge consensus
  labeling pipeline (`eval/consensus/results/consensus_labels.jsonl`, PLAN.md item B) scores
  inter-judge agreement on a 132-fixture CI-gate-adjacent set, not the held-out corpus, and its
  agreement counts don't correspond to "wrong-committed" in any field either.
- Checked for any session-brief archive or external notes file that might hold the original
  citation — none exists in this repository; every session's brief arrives as chat input and is
  not itself committed anywhere.

**Conclusion: no artifact in this repository's full history — on any branch — ever contained the
figures "buy_again 0/10" or "sentiment 1/12" as wrong-committed counts.** The most plausible
explanation is that these were a verbally-approximated or misremembered figure from outside this
repo's committed record (a different session's spoken summary, rounded or reconstructed from
memory rather than read off an artifact) — not a measurement this project ever produced and later
lost. This is stated as the most plausible explanation, not a verified fact about what happened in
a conversation this repo has no record of; it is not being presented as more certain than the
evidence (an exhaustive, negative search) supports.

## What IS real and reconcilable

ADR 0018's n=23 figures and ADR 0026's n=106 figures — both real, both artifact-backed, both
already cross-checked against each other in ADR 0026 (CIs overlap; n=106 didn't move the needle,
it tightened it). Re-ran `eval/analyze_coverage_metrics.py` this session for a second independent
confirmation: **byte-identical output to ADR 0026's numbers, zero quota spent** (cassette-replay,
no network call). The n=106 table stands, unchanged:

| Field | Coverage | Accuracy-on-answered | Wrong-committed |
|---|---|---|---|
| sentiment | 77.4% [68.9%, 84.9%] | 87.8% [80.5%, 93.9%] | 10/82 = 12.2% [6.1%, 19.5%] |
| buy_again | 43.4% [34.0%, 52.8%] | 76.1% [63.0%, 87.0%] | 11/46 = 23.9% [13.0%, 37.0%] |

n=106, `as_deployed` condition (real language routing), bootstrap 95% CIs (seed=42, 10,000
resamples). Source: `eval/results/coverage_metrics_n106.json`, generated by
`eval/analyze_coverage_metrics.py` from `eval/results/held_out_scoring_v2.json`.

## P1b — the headline claim, stated with its CI

Per-field, matching ADR 0026's decision not to blend fields with materially different rates:

> **When it commits to an answer, this model is correct 87.8% of the time for sentiment (95% CI
> 80.5–93.9%, n=106) and 76.1% of the time for buy_again (95% CI 63.0–87.0%, n=106) — the two
> fields diverge enough that a single blended "rarely wrong when it commits" claim would
> misrepresent buy_again specifically.**

This does **not** support publishing "rarely wrong when it commits" as a standalone, field-
agnostic headline. It supports two field-scoped claims, each carrying its own number and its own
CI. **Accuracy-on-answered and abstention rate are two independent, separately-measured
quantities, not complements of each other** — a wrong-when-committed outcome and an abstention are
different behaviors, so a claim must not describe one as happening "instead of" the other. Stated
correctly, as two separate facts: sentiment is answered 77.4% of the time (95% CI [68.9%, 84.9%])
and correct 87.8% of those times (95% CI [80.5%, 93.9%]); buy_again is answered 43.4% of the time
(95% CI [34.0%, 52.8%]) and correct 76.1% of those times (95% CI [63.0%, 87.0%]). If the landing
page needs one sentence, it must keep these as two clauses, not one: *"When this model commits to
a sentiment or buy-again verdict it's right roughly 8 in 10 times (field-specific rates and CIs
above) — and for buy_again in particular, it abstains rather than guess on more than half of all
reviews (56.6% abstention, 95% CI [47.2%, 66.0%])."*

## Decision

- The n=23 "0/10"/"1/12" figures are retired from this project's record — they do not correspond
  to any measurement this repo can produce or verify, and will not be cited again.
- The n=106 table (ADR 0026, reconfirmed here) is the current canonical selling metric.
- The headline claim above is the exact sentence P6's sales page, pricing page, and any outreach
  copy must use — per-field, with its CI, never blended.
- Added `committed_accuracy_headline` to the metrics-injection system (`scripts/
  render_metrics.py`) so this sentence is generated from `eval/results/coverage_metrics_n106.json`
  and cannot drift from the underlying numbers the way a hand-typed claim could — same discipline
  as every other public metric in this repo (rule 65c).

## Consequences

- Nobody has to re-litigate this discrepancy again — the search was exhaustive (full git history,
  not just current files) and the conclusion (no source exists) is as strong as a negative result
  can be stated.
- The headline claim is now generated, not hand-typed — a future change to the underlying JSON
  will regenerate this sentence automatically via `render_metrics.py`, and `--check` will fail if
  it's ever left stale.

## Alternatives considered

- **Assume "0/10"/"1/12" refers to some subset of the corpus not yet identified and keep
  searching indefinitely.** Rejected: the search already covered every plausible source in this
  repository (full history, every branch, every related JSON/consensus artifact); further
  searching without a new lead would not be evidence-gathering, it would be speculation.
- **Publish a single blended "rarely wrong when it commits" claim anyway, since it's a stronger
  marketing line.** Rejected outright — ADR 0026 already established this doesn't hold for
  buy_again, and publishing it would be exactly the kind of claim this project's own rules (rule
  65a: never blur "verified" with "believed") exist to prevent.
