# ADR 0032: the held-out corpus was exposed, and stored panel splits as labels

Status: accepted (Session 16, V1/V2). Supersedes the "never seen by anyone iterating on the
prompt" claim in ADR 0016 and the README section that repeated it. Every number below is from
`eval/results/held_out_scoring_v2.json` (regenerated by cassette replay, $0, no model output
changed) unless a command is named.

## Context

Two questions were open. (V1) 21 of the held-out reviews also appear in
`benchmark/dataset/gold.jsonl`: did that ever inform a prompt? (V2) `build_held_out_corpus`
stored a judge-panel split as a default (`"unknown"`, `[]`, `null`) that is indistinguishable from
a genuine empty label: 30 `pros`, 26 `product`, 25 `topics`, 21 `cons` golds were defaults.

## Findings (V1) -- VERIFIED unless marked

1. **The overlap is 36 reviews of 106, not 21, from two development sets, with zero reviews in
   both.** Whole-review match after normalization (lowercase, alphanumerics only, the scraper's
   "READ MORE" removed):
   * 21 = **every** review in the benchmark's `hi-en` slice (`bench-hien-001..021`);
   * 15 = **every** review in the prompt-visible `eval/fixtures/hi-en/001..015.json`, the CI-gate
     set the `hi_en` prompt has been developed and regression-gated against since v2.0
     (commit `e57baee`: "80.6% PASS (15/15 fixtures)").
2. **Mechanism.** `build_candidate_queue` excluded candidates already in the quarantine directory
   and nothing else. The candidate pool (`eval/data/flipkart_candidates.jsonl`, 106 Roman-script
   Hinglish reviews) is the same pool the dev fixtures and the benchmark slice were sampled from
   earlier, so "take every remaining candidate" swallowed all of both. It was systematic, not
   chance.
3. **No verbatim copy reached a prompt.** Scanning all 23 historical revisions of
   `app/core/prompts/**`, `app/core/prompt.py` and `PROMPTS.md` for any 40-character window of
   any held-out or benchmark review found only `hien-0057` = "Mast product" (19 characters, a
   generic phrase) and `bench-en-004` quoted in `PROMPTS.md` (en slice, not held-out).
4. **But paraphrase did.** All four few-shot examples in `app/core/prompts/hi_en.py` are rewrites
   of dev fixtures that are also held-out reviews: "Superb earphone, sound ekdum mast hai yaar"
   -> `hi-en/001` (`hien-0081`); "Pros: Good sound quality(not much better than a cheap local
   earphone)" -> `hi-en/012` (`hien-0075`); "Ekdum mast product hai bhai" -> `hi-en/015`
   (`hien-0052`); "Product quality bahut achha hai, lekin delivery wala time pe nahi aaya" ->
   `hi-en/014` (`hien-0073`). Found by reading the examples beside the fixtures, not by any
   control: `scripts/check_no_heldout_leakage.py` compared only the first 40 characters of each
   review, only against prompt text, and its own docstring named paraphrase as out of scope.
5. **The benchmark path is indirect.** Prompt v2.2 (`2ec83f5`) and v2.3 (`13a4422`) cite the
   benchmark's adjudicated labels; v2.3 targets `bench-en-013` by id. Those are `en`-slice cases
   and none is in the held-out set. v2.2 also rewrote `hi_en.py`'s urgency rubric and flipped two
   example urgencies, and its acceptance metric (`URG/_all` 68.5% -> 79.2%) included the 21 `hi-en`
   items (BELIEVED: `_all` is the en + hi-en slices; the commit message does not itemize it);
   Target A (`d87c3e5`, 2026-07-06) inspected each of the 9 disagreements on the 21 `hi-en` items
   individually and decided *not* to change the prompt. `git log -S"bench-hien"` shows exactly those three commits.
   Neither `urgency` nor `language` is in the held-out headline; `sentiment` is.
6. **Effect on the headline is not detectable, and is not proven absent.** As deployed, split gold
   excluded, exposed reviews (n=36) score 80.7% vs 79.5% for unseen (n=70): difference +1.2 pp,
   95% CI [-4.2, +6.4] (unpaired bootstrap, seed 42, `exposure_sensitivity` in the artifact).

## Findings (V1e): every other place held-out text appears

Sweep of all 844 git-tracked text files (JSON/JSONL decoded first so escapes cannot hide a match),
whole-review and 40-character-window containment, for the 89 of 106 held-out reviews long enough
(>= 30 normalized characters) to match without false positives; the 17 shorter ones were not
swept by this method. Files with hits, other than the quarantine directory itself:
`benchmark/dataset/{gold,candidates_for_review}.jsonl` and `benchmark/results/*` (the 21);
`eval/fixtures/hi-en/001..015.json` (the 15); `eval/consensus/results/consensus_labels.jsonl` (the
judge-panel run over the dev fixtures, not a prompt input); `eval/cassettes/{held_out,
buy_again_exp}_cassettes.json` and `eval/results/*` (the held-out runs themselves, by design);
`site/demo-data.json` (`hien-0081`, shown in the public demo); two specs quoting a review; and
`tests/unit/test_language.py`, `tests/unit/test_sample_flipkart_language_detection.py` (the
language detector was tested against pool reviews, so routing, unlike the labels, has seen this
text). Not swept: the 14,552-row source datasets under `eval/data/raw*` (the pool itself).

## Decision

1. **The published held-out headline excludes the 36 exposed reviews and the split gold pairs.**
   As deployed, headline fields (`stars` and `language` excluded as before), from the same recorded
   outputs:

   | reviews | gold pairs | score | 95% CI | n |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | all | all | 72.4% | [69.8, 75.0] | 106 |
   | all | split excluded | 79.9% | [77.3, 82.5] | 106 |
   | unseen only | all | 73.2% | [69.9, 76.5] | 70 |
   | **unseen only** | **split excluded** | **79.5%** | **[76.0, 82.7]** | **70** |

   **Which is honest:** the last row. The first row is a real measurement of something else (all
   106 with default labels scored as labels); excluding the exposed reviews *raises* it 0.8 pp
   rather than lowering it, which is what a contaminated set benefiting from exposure would not
   do. That is why this is a scoping fix, not a correction of a flattering number: the exposure
   did not measurably help, but a held-out claim must not depend on that. Splitting out the split
   gold moves the headline **up** 7.5 pp (the earlier +6.3 pp was on the pre-D7 field set that
   still averaged `language`); per field it moves `pros` 53.9 -> 73.8, `cons` 70.1 -> 87.4,
   `topics` 54.2 -> 68.5 and `product` **59.4 -> 57.5 (down)**. The rise is the panel's disagreement
   no longer being scored as the model's error; it is not a model improvement.
2. **The builder stores a split distinguishably.** `build_fixture` writes
   `labeling_meta.unresolved_fields` (fields whose agreement is not unanimous/majority, or absent).
   The schema-valid default stays in `ground_truth`. The 106 committed fixtures are not rewritten:
   `eval/heldout_exposure.py::unresolved_fields` derives the same list from `agreement_per_field`,
   and a test pins that the two rules agree. The builder also now excludes candidates already in the
   dev fixtures or the benchmark gold.
3. **A ledger and a guard.** `eval/heldout_exposure_ack.json` lists the 36 with their reasons and
   the 4 hand-found few-shot sources. `scripts/check_no_heldout_leakage.py` (already in CI) now
   fails on an overlap not in the ledger and on a ledger entry that is no longer an overlap, and
   fails closed if the ledger is missing. Acknowledging an id does not return it to the headline.
4. **What was not done.** No gold label was relabelled (hien-0065 "backup", hien-0070 "Osm
   products" remain; see the draft PR). The `product` policy ("infer a category when there is a
   cue") was **not** implemented: it is a prompt change that needs a re-record and, measured on
   this corpus, would tune the prompt on the corpus that is supposed to measure it. It needs an
   independent set first.

## Consequences

* The headline number goes up, the n goes down (106 -> 70), the CI width is about the same. Both
  directions are printed in the README block, rendered from the artifact (rule 65c).
* The `hi_en` prompt's few-shot examples are derived from held-out reviews; they are not removed
  here (a prompt change needs its own eval). Any future hi-en measurement should treat the four as
  exposed and, better, replace them with reviews from outside the pool.
* Coverage/abstention figures (`coverage_metrics_n106.json`) use only `sentiment` and `buy_again`,
  which have **0** split pairs, so they were never computed over default labels. They *are*
  computed over all 106 reviews including the 36 exposed; they are not re-cut here.
* What this still does not catch: paraphrase (found four by hand), and any exposure through the
  language detector's tuning.

## Alternatives

* Keep all 106 in the headline and disclose the overlap: rejected, the claim is "held out".
* Relabel the split fields by adjudication: rejected, it is 102 pairs of LLM-judge disagreement and
  relabelling to a higher score is the contamination pattern.
* Rebuild the corpus from a fresh pool: correct in the long run, but the pool has no reviews left
  that the dev sets have not seen; needs a new source.
