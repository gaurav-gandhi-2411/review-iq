# ADR 0033: the held-out headline is accuracy where the panel agreed; the unscored fraction goes beside it

Status: accepted (Session 17, W3). Extends ADR 0032; corrects figures in ADR 0021, 0023, 0026,
0027, 0030 and 0032 by dated notes (see Consequences). Every number below is from the committed
artifacts named in the table headings, regenerated by cassette replay and offline analysis
(`$0`, no model call, no model output changed), at base commit `8186d0d` (PR #236 rebased,
including #240). Nothing here was typed from memory.

## Context

ADR 0032 stopped scoring panel-split gold defaults as labels and excluded the 36 reviews the
development process had seen. The headline became 79.6% [76.2, 82.8] on n=70
(`eval/results/held_out_scoring_v2.json`, `headline_grid.unexposed_split_excluded`).

**The brief for this work quoted 79.5% [76.0, 82.7].** That does not reproduce. It was the
committed artifact at `f33ed9d` (79.49 [76.03, 82.70], 59 split pairs); #240 moved `hien-0065`
and `hien-0070` `product` to unresolved (60 pairs) and the headline to 79.63 [76.24, 82.77]. The
prose in ADR 0032 and `docs/eval-contamination-and-the-honest-number.md` was never refreshed.
Replaying the cassettes at the tip reproduces 79.6 [76.2, 82.8] byte for byte apart from
`generated_at`/`git_sha`; that is what is published now.

Excluding split pairs is right, but it is **also a selection effect**. A pair is split when three
judges could not agree, which is where a review is hardest or most ambiguous. The score on the
remaining pairs is accuracy *where the panel agreed*, not overall accuracy. The size of the effect
is visible in ADR 0032's own table: `pros` reads 53.9% with defaults scored and 73.8% with them
excluded. Publishing the bare number overstated the product, and nothing beside it said so.

Four other analyses read the same gold and were never checked for the same defect (W3e), and
the 17 shortest held-out reviews were outside the V1e exposure sweep (W3f).

## Decision

1. **The headline is published with the unscored fraction beside it**, from the artifact, in the
   one sentence: *79.6% [76.2, 82.8] on 70 unseen reviews, scored where the three-judge panel
   reached consensus; 10.7% of field-pairs (60 of 560) had no consensus and are unscored.* A
   **field-pair** is one (review, headline field) cell on the published cell:
   70 reviews x 8 fields = 560. It is **unscored** when the field is in the review's
   `unresolved_fields` (agreement not unanimous/majority). `eval/heldout_unscored.py` computes it,
   `eval/score_held_out_corpus_v2.py::summarize` writes it into the artifact (`unscored`), and
   `scripts/render_metrics.py` renders it into the README `held_out_table` block (rule 65c).

   | buy_again | 0 / 70 | 0.0% | 67.1% | 67.1% | 67.1% |

   46 of the 70 reviews have at least one unscored pair; none has every pair unscored. The
   unscored mass sits entirely in the four free-text fields, which are the hardest ones.
2. **No point estimate of overall accuracy is identifiable, and the artifact says so**
   (`point_estimate_identifiable: false`). Whether an unscored pair is answered correctly is the
   unobserved quantity, and it is unobserved because the panel disagreed, which plausibly
   correlates with the model being wrong. The only assumption-free statement is the Manski
   interval (every unscored pair scored all wrong / all right, per-review mean, the headline's
   unit): **[70.9%, 81.6%]** (bootstrap 95% CI of the endpoints [67.6, 74.0] and [78.4, 84.5]).
   It is rendered as a bound and labelled one; it is not a headline. Imputing the unscored pairs
   at the accuracy of "easy" strata was rejected: the missingness is driven by difficulty, so that
   assumption builds the answer in.
3. **A conditional sensitivity is published, labelled as such.** Under the assumption that the
   true label of an unscored pair is one of the three judges' answers (UNVERIFIED; all three can
   be wrong), scoring each unscored pair against each judge in turn gives 74.3%, 74.8% and 75.6%
   overall (qwen3.6, qwen3.8, gemini), and the per-pair worst/best over the judges gives
   [73.2%, 77.3%]. Every one of these is below 79.6%, by 4.0 to 5.3 pp for the single-judge
   readings. Reading: **the headline probably overstates overall accuracy by about 4-5 pp, and
   the data cannot say by exactly how much.** The default scored as a label (73.2% on n=70) is in
   the artifact only as a reference; it is not a bound and not an estimate. The votes come from
   the last entry per fixture in `eval/consensus/results/held_out_batch_log.jsonl` (40 fixtures
   were re-labelled); `validate_votes_match_gold` fails the run unless a vote equals the stored
   gold on every resolved field, and it passes on all 106.

## Consequences

* The headline stays 79.6%, but it no longer stands alone. Published beside it: 10.7% of
  field-pairs unscored (22.9% / 21.4% / 24.3% / 17.1% on product / topics / pros / cons), the
  interval [70.9, 81.6] as a bound, and a conditional estimate 4-5 pp lower. The honest summary is
  "about 80% where the panel agreed, probably around 75% overall, unidentifiable exactly".
* Two published claims went **down**: buy_again coverage and accuracy-on-answered. They are
  disclosed in the same change as the one that held steady (sentiment accuracy 87.8 -> 88.0) and
  the one that fell (sentiment coverage 77.4 -> 71.4).
* ADR 0027's sales sentence ("right roughly 8 in 10 times ... buy_again ... abstains on more than
  half of all reviews (56.6%)") was computed on the 106; on the unseen 70 buy_again abstains on
  75.7% of reviews and is right 70.6% of the time when it commits, on 17 answers (CI 47.1 to 88.2).
  That sentence must not be reused until it is rewritten from the artifact.
* A point estimate needs an independent adjudication of the 60 unscored pairs by a native
  Hinglish reader. That needs GG; an LLM relabelling would be the contamination pattern.
* Still not covered: paraphrase exposure beyond the four hand-found few-shots; exposure through
  the language detector's tuning; the demo page shows `hien-0081` (a few-shot source) as a "real
  review" example (not an accuracy claim, left as is); `SECURITY.md` says the prompt-guard
  classifier had "never seen" the 106 (a different model, BELIEVED unaffected, not re-checked).

## Alternatives

* **Publish the Manski interval as the headline**: rejected. Its lower end assumes every
  unscored pair is wrong, which no one believes; it is a bound, and is shown as one.
* **Impute the unscored pairs from the scored ones**: rejected as invalid; the missingness is
  not random, it is the difficulty signal.
* **Relabel the unscored pairs with another LLM pass or a majority of two**: rejected, same
  reason as ADR 0032; relabelling toward a higher score is the contamination pattern.
* **Drop the held-out headline**: rejected. It is the only number measured on text development
  had not seen; the fix is to qualify it.
* **Re-cut the coverage figures but keep the all-106 numbers as the headline**: rejected; the
  exposed reviews are not a neutral sample (buy_again coverage 80.6% on them against 24.3%).


## Update 2026-10-07 (S18 D1/D2): the published sentence now carries the second-panel read, and `pros` is reported separately

The sentence this ADR specified ("... scored where the three-judge panel reached consensus; 10.7% ... unscored.") is superseded in the README `held_out_table` block by the approved wording: *"79.6% [76.2%, 82.8%] on 70 unseen reviews, scored only where the three-judge panel agreed. 10.7% of field-pairs (60 of 560) had no consensus and are unscored. Those are the hardest cases: on the 29 of them a second, independent LLM panel could settle, the model scores 47-71% depending on the field. So overall accuracy is probably lower than the headline, in roughly the mid-70s (exploratory, LLM-judged, not human-verified)."* Every figure is rendered from `held_out_scoring_v2.json` and `eval/consensus/results/panel2_silver.json` (ADR 0034); the 47-71% range is the min-max of the rendered per-field second-panel scores (rounded to integers), and the 29 is the artifact resolved count. "Roughly the mid-70s" is guarded in `scripts/render_metrics.py`: it is printed only while the three panel-1 judge-sensitivity scores and the narrowed Manski lower bound all lie in 73.0-78.0% and below the headline, and the silver estimate lies in [73.0%, headline); otherwise the render fails with a message. The narrowed Manski interval itself stays in ADR 0034 and the artifacts and is not printed in the README or the site. The silver estimate (78.05%) sits 0.05 points above the 78.0% edge, so it is held to the one-sided condition above, not the band; that choice is disclosed in PR for S18.

**Change 2026-10-07 (D6, GG decision): "in roughly the mid-70s" is replaced by a stated range.** The sentence now ends "...So overall accuracy is probably lower than the headline — independent estimates range 73.7% to 78.0% (exploratory, LLM-judged, not human-verified)." Both endpoints are rendered from `panel2_silver.json` by `scripts/render_metrics.py` (narrowed Manski lower bound; silver adjudicated estimate), never typed. The mid-70s band guard (`MidSeventiesGuardError`, `mid_seventies_clause`) and its tests were deleted: a guard that exists to protect a phrase is the wrong shape. The replacement guard checks the endpoints: both must be read from the artifact (error if missing), lower < upper, and both strictly below the headline point estimate, otherwise the build fails with a message naming the values. The narrowed Manski UPPER bound and the pair "[73.7%, 79.3%]" still never appear in the README or site; the lower endpoint now legitimately appears as the left end of the stated range. The silver endpoint renders as 78.0%, not 78.1%: the artifact value is 0.78046, and the "78.05%" above is that value pre-rounded to two decimals (rounding it again would overstate it). The paragraph above describes the superseded wording and guard.

Also in this change: `pros` soft recall at a pre-registered 0.5 token-Jaccard threshold is reported beside the headline, not inside it (`docs/specs/s18-pros-soft-recall.md`, additive block `pros_soft_recall`). Validation V-a and V-b both FAILED, so the field is reported as not validated and the headline keeps its existing scorer. The unqualified sentence "No point estimate of overall accuracy is identifiable" is now "No assumption-free point estimate ..." because the exploratory second-panel read above is an estimate under stated assumptions.
