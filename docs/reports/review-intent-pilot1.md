# Review intent: agreement pilot 1 (S22 C3)

Gate for Part 3 (`docs/specs/review-intent.md` section 5). 300 reviews (75 apparel, 75 food, 75 beauty, 75
Hinglish marketplace; the Hinglish stratum is oversampled), four judges from four families run blind on Kaggle
T4s (llama3.1:8b Meta, qwen3:8b Alibaba, gemma2:9b Google, mistral:7b Mistral), prompt `ri-judge-v1`, commit
`54efcee`. Results: `reports/labelling/pilot1/` (judge files carry ids and labels only; the review text
stays in a private Kaggle dataset). Metrics are generated by `engine/labelling/agreement.py`; diagnostics by
`engine/labelling/pilot_diag.py`. These are SILVER labels (LLM consensus), not ground truth.

## 1. Verdict

1. No task reaches the pre-registered PASS (alpha 0.80 and consensus 0.80). Three reach USABLE-WITH-CAVEAT:
   primary intent (alpha 0.693), sentiment (0.772), urgency (0.716).
2. Three FAIL as defined: buy_again (0.416), rating-text mismatch (0.392), aspect presence
   (0.23 to 0.33).
3. The bigger problem for the 95 percent claim is SUPPORT, not agreement: the consensus primary intent is
   `praise` for 193 of 300 reviews and `product_defect` for 57; every other class has 7 or fewer.
   A precision claim for `delivery`, `customer_service`, `question`, `competitor_comparison` and
   `spam_irrelevant` cannot be measured on English product-review corpora; those classes live in the
   customers' own feeds (Judge.me, marketplace Q and A), which is what the connector is for.
4. The clear-consensus fraction is the ceiling on coverage for each task (table below). A claim at coverage
   above it has no labels to be measured against.

## 3. What the judges disagree about

- T1 intent: the dominant confusions are `praise` vs `suggestion` (41 judge pairs), `praise` vs `product_defect`
  (36), `praise` vs `pricing` (32), `product_defect` vs `return_refund_request` (29). That is the
  "one primary intent" rule failing on mixed reviews ("love it, wish it were cheaper"), not noise.
- Judge style: gemma2 labels 30 reviews `suggestion` where llama3.1 labels 3 and mistral 4; qwen3 labels 220
  `praise` and 8 `spam_irrelevant`. Pairwise T1 agreement between any two judges is 0.82 to 0.87, so no one
  judge is an outlier on T1.
- T4 buy_again: 480 of the 645 pairwise disagreements are `unclear` vs `yes`: judges differ on whether an
  implied recommendation ("love it") counts. Almost none are `yes` vs `no`.
- T5 mismatch: dropping qwen3 moves alpha from 0.392 to 0.658; one judge reads the task differently. The judges find
  54 consensus mismatches in 257; a derived rule finds 10 in 275 (stars 4 to 5 with negative
  consensus sentiment, or stars 1 to 2 with positive). The judges are answering a different question.
- T3 urgency: `low` vs `medium` (166 pairs) dominates; `high` has 5 consensus items.
- Panel health: text-call parse failures llama 2, gemma 0, qwen 1, mistral 20
  (6.7 percent of mistral's items); mismatch-call failures all 0. A failed judge counts as missing for that
  item, not as a vote.

## 4. Is a 0.95-precision claim measurable, task by task

What IS measurable here is precision against the panel's consensus labels, at coverage up to the clear-consensus
fraction. Precision against the truth needs a human audit of the consensus set, which this pilot does not have.
One external check does exist: the star rating, never shown to the judges for the text tasks. Consensus sentiment
vs stars: 1 to 2 stars {'negative': 32, 'positive': 2}; 4 to 5 stars {'positive': 198, 'mixed': 6, 'negative': 8}. Only 2 of 203 consensus-positive items sit at 1 to 2
stars, which bounds label noise from the one direction stars can check; 8 of 62 consensus-negative items sit at 4 to 5 stars,
which are plausibly the genuine mismatch cases rather than judge errors.

| Task | Measurable now? | Definition fix proposed (not applied; needs GG's view) |
|---|---|---|
| T1 intent | Only praise and product_defect | Priority rule: if a review contains any actionable complaint it is not `praise`; order ACTION over INSIGHT over SIGNAL; add 3 worked examples per confusable pair. Rare classes need targeted sampling (candidate mining on the customers' feeds), not more random English reviews |
| T2 sentiment | Yes (best task) | None. Keep. `neutral` never reaches consensus, so drop it or merge it into `mixed` |
| T3 urgency | low vs medium | Add boundary examples for medium vs low; `high` needs targeted sampling (safety, harm, escalation reviews) |
| T4 buy_again | No as 3-way | Binary `explicit_no` (the reviewer says they will not repurchase or recommend): alpha 0.700, consensus 0.933 on the same pilot labels |
| T5 mismatch | No as judged | Derived, not judged: stars vs consensus sentiment. Reliability equals T2's |
| T6 aspects | Presence no; sentiment where co-mentioned yes | Require the judge to quote the supporting span and accept presence only when the quote is a substring of the review (mechanically checkable); shrink each list to the 5 most discussed aspects |
